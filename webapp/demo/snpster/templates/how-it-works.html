Chapter four

SNPster standardizes the genetic data

Consumer testing companies do not all use the same file structure, genome build, strand orientation, or set of measured variants. SNPster first converts the uploaded data into a consistent internal format.

The pipeline validates chromosomes and positions, normalizes genotype representation, converts genome coordinates when necessary, and removes records that cannot be interpreted safely.

  • Detect the vendor-specific file format
  • Normalize chromosome and genotype values
  • Convert variants to a common genome build
  • Remove invalid or ambiguous records

Chapter five

SNPster imputes millions of additional variants

A consumer microarray measures only a fraction of the common variants found throughout the genome. Imputation uses patterns from a genetic reference panel to statistically infer additional variants that were not directly measured by the vendor.

Every inferred genotype has uncertainty. SNPster keeps confidence information and applies quality filters so that poorly supported variants are excluded from later calculations.

  • Compare measured variants with a reference panel
  • Infer unmeasured variants statistically
  • Retain genotype probability and dosage information
  • Remove variants below the quality threshold

Chapter six

SNPster calculates published polygenic scores

After imputation and quality control, SNPster compares Alex's processed variants with published scoring files. Each score contains genetic variants and weights derived from a scientific study.

SNPster calculates multiple scores for the same trait when suitable published models are available. It also records how many score variants were matched and which evaluation statistics were reported by the original authors.

  • Match processed variants to published PGS models
  • Calculate the weighted genetic score
  • Normalize the result against reference populations
  • Record variant match and model performance data

Final chapter

Alex receives the final SNPster report

Once all calculations are complete, Alex can open a report organized by trait. Instead of showing only one number, SNPster presents the available models, their agreement, their performance, and the quality of the underlying match.

The report is designed to explain uncertainty. A high percentile does not automatically mean that someone will develop a condition, and a low percentile does not guarantee protection.

  • Review percentile results by trait
  • Compare agreement across multiple PGS models
  • Inspect AUROC, concordance, odds, and hazard ratios
  • Review ancestry context and variant match quality

What the report means

A statistical result, not a diagnosis

SNPster helps organize and explain published polygenic scores. It does not diagnose disease, predict the future with certainty, or replace medical advice.

Polygenic scores describe statistical differences between people within the context of the populations and studies used to create and evaluate each model.

Model agreement matters

Multiple scores for the same trait may produce different percentiles. The report highlights where models agree and where they differ.

Performance varies

AUROC and concordance statistics describe how well models separated outcomes in their evaluation data.

Ancestry affects interpretation

Score performance can differ across populations, especially when the original research data lacked diverse ancestry representation.

Lifestyle still matters

Genetics is only one component of health. Age, environment, behavior, exposures, and clinical factors may be equally or more important.
